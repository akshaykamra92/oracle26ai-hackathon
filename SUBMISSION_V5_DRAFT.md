# AI Judge version 5 draft — one-opening decision wedge

Version 4 (Run #3184) scored 91.7. Solution Fit & Product Design remains the clearest improvement opportunity at 75/100; the judge asked for the smallest useful workflow before the wider platform vision. This version makes one RN opening and one reviewer-ready decision the entry point, while preserving the tested demo, market evidence, and honest pilot target.

The **50% time reduction remains an unvalidated pilot target**. The counts below come from fictional fixtures, not customer operations.

## What did you build?

The smallest useful product workflow is a pre-approval check for one proposed RN unit: one central planner enters four demand minima and receives one source-checked opening recommendation for source-manager review. Can We Staff It? demonstrates that workflow in a public browser without uncovering existing sites. Choose a seeded case or set total, senior, lead, and specialty demand, then click Evaluate workforce. The result ties an executive go/wait/gap recommendation to the exact move sequence, a before-and-after source-floor view, and reasons for blocked candidates. The Tampa case shows an RN backfill cascade; Orlando shows a ready nonlicensed service hub; Phoenix shows how a possible promotion changes senior mix without creating another person. The new one-click case buttons and transfer panel make the first useful decision visible within one screen of the recommendation.

## Who has this problem?

The first user is a central workforce planner at a regional multi-site health system; the system workforce or operations leader is the buyer hypothesis. Today the planner exports rosters, translates RN I/RN II/Charge RN codes, checks license and mobility records, asks source managers whether each promising transfer would breach local coverage, and rebuilds the shortlist after a backfill changes another site. A qualified employee is not necessarily transferable. AHA's 2025 health-systems snapshot reports 397 U.S. health systems, and its 2026 hospital Fast Facts reports 3,567 community hospitals in systems. Those are reach signals from different snapshots, not customers or a revenue estimate; only a subset fits our initial multi-site segment. Our first adoption test is one RN opening with one central planner and source-manager review. We have no measured customer time or cost yet.

## How does your app solve it?

One planner, one RN opening, four demand numbers, one reviewable answer: this is the first workflow to pilot. The answer can be "open under these modeled rules" or "one more qualified person is needed"; either gives source managers a concrete proposal to review before transfers. The product follows the planner's painful steps. (1) Instead of reconciling a new spreadsheet for every opening, the planner chooses a case and enters four demand numbers; RN job codes are treated as one family while senior and charge minima remain distinct. (2) Instead of manually screening a shortlist, the planner sees each candidate accepted or blocked by skills, location/opt-in, destination-state RN authority, and the proposed date. (3) Instead of discovering the downstream vacancy through calls, the app tests every source floor and shows backfills in the order they must occur. In fictional Tampa, RN-01 can leave Orlando West because 7 RNs become 6 against a floor of 6; RN-12 then backfills its charge role before RN-02 moves. The transfer panel shows that sequence and the source's 7→6 total, 2→1 senior, and 1→1 lead mix. (4) Instead of rewriting the recommendation, the planner edits demand and immediately sees the gap, conditional career checkpoint, and next action. The fixture evaluates 11 proposals, blocks 4, sequences 1 backfill, and leaves 0 modeled source-floor breaches; Tampa still needs one qualified RN. These are repeatable demo results. In a 10-case deidentified pilot, our unvalidated target is at least 50% less median time to a reviewer-accepted, policy-compliant plan versus the same team's manual baseline, with zero source-floor breaches in accepted plans.

## How did you build it?

The first adoption test is a single RN opening and a human review of its transfer plan; the public Orlando and Phoenix cases demonstrate a later expansion path. The live GitHub Pages app is static HTML, CSS, and JavaScript with fictional rosters and a deterministic in-browser planner; there is no runtime AI model or Oracle database. For each candidate the planner checks job family/level, required skill, geography or opt-in, destination-state RN authority, and total/senior/lead source floors. It searches short local backfill chains and applies the backfill before the destination move. The UI exposes that causal chain, source before→after mix, blocked reasons, three one-click cases, instant recomputation, and a primary button that scrolls to the recommendation. Career checkpoints require tenure, rating at least 3/5, recorded skills, approval, and continued availability. Six planner tests plus live browser checks cover the seeded cases and the Phoenix +1 stress test. Other job families and secure roster/credential integrations are later steps. Candidate ranking alone cannot show transferable capacity after the cascade. Customer-specific validated policies and decision history could become a durable advantage, but neither is established yet. Production requires live HRIS, license, schedule, approved policy, access-control, audit, and human sign-off workflows.
