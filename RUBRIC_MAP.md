# AI Judge rubric map — evidence, not a predicted score

The signed-in [hackathon portal](https://app.hacktheworldaway.org/) showed the following five categories during the team's review. This map ties each category to what the current demo can actually show.

| Category | Weight | Current evidence | What to say honestly |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Demand Reality & Problem Severity | 25% | Tampa evaluates 11 synthetic transfer proposals, blocks 4, sequences 1 backfill, and ends with 0 modeled source-floor breaches and 1 honest destination gap. [AHA](https://www.aha.org/system/files/media/file/2025/12/2026-Health-Care-Workforce-Scan-Executive-Summary.pdf) and [AHRQ](https://psnet.ahrq.gov/primer/nursing-and-patient-safety) support the importance of healthcare workforce planning and skill mix. | These are demo outputs, not measured customer results. A pilot would measure time to a valid plan and conflicts caught before approval. |
| Target Customer & Market Scope | 20% | First user: central workforce planner at a multi-facility health system; first buyer hypothesis: system-level workforce or operations leader. [AHA Fast Facts](https://www.aha.org/statistics/fast-facts-us-hospitals) reports 3,567 U.S. community hospitals in a health system, a reach signal for the U.S. healthcare wedge. Orlando and Phoenix show a nonlicensed family. | That count is market context, not potential contracts, users, traction, or willingness to pay. Other industries are hypotheses. |
| Solution Fit & Product Design | 20% | A judge can set demand, see the executive answer and counted checks, inspect each move and source stack, then use one click to stress-test Phoenix headcount and reset. | The rules and data are synthetic. A planner would validate inputs and obtain approval in a real workflow. |
| Technical Execution & Demo Proof | 20% | Public [live app](https://akshaykamra92.github.io/oracle26ai-hackathon/) and [source](https://github.com/akshaykamra92/oracle26ai-hackathon), three reproducible scenarios, deterministic chain and forecast logic, and core automated checks. Tampa: 7/8 with one backfill and four blocks. Orlando: 6/6 ready. Phoenix: 6/6 headcount, role mix first ready at +24-month checkpoint. | The shipped app has no live AI model, Oracle database, production roster, or clinically validated staffing rule. Check any sponsor technology requirement before submitting. |
| Differentiation & Investment Readiness | 15% | The demo's specific advantage over a simple vacancy or skills list is a network-level answer: it calculates the vacancy caused by a move, orders feasible backfills, shows rejected moves, and tests whether career progression changes readiness. | Do not claim uniqueness or traction. Future defensibility would depend on validated policy configurations, integrations, and an audit history built with customers. |

## Suggested judge demo order

1. **Tampa:** Show the network consequence. Explain why 7 → 6 is allowed, then show RN-12 backfilling RN-02's charge role. Point to a blocked source move, mobility and license blocks, and the one-person gap.
2. **Orlando:** Switch to the nonlicensed service hub. Its senior bench makes the fictional opening ready now.
3. **Phoenix:** Show the opposite executive signal: enough headcount, too few senior and lead people. The career ladder gives a **conditional** +24-month checkpoint. Click **Try Phoenix +1 role** to show that a seven-person target remains short because promotion does not create headcount, then reset.

The sharpest problem statement is: **“When a multi-site organization opens a new service, who can actually move without uncovering another team, and when will the right role mix exist?”**
