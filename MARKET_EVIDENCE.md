# Market evidence and claim boundaries

This page keeps public market context separate from the prototype's synthetic results and the pilot outcome we have not measured. It supports a narrower first buyer story: a central workforce planner and workforce/operations leader at a regional multi-site health system making one RN unit-opening decision.

## Primary signals

| Signal | What the source says | How we can use it | What it does not prove |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| U.S. health systems | [AHA Fast Facts: U.S. Health Systems 2025](https://www.aha.org/infographics/2025-06-18-fast-facts-us-health-systems-2025-infographic) reports **397 systems**. Its definition includes multi-hospital systems and diversified single-hospital systems. | An upper context count for organizations where central planning may exist. The first target is only the multi-site subset. | 397 customers, purchasable accounts, or a validated market size. |
| System-affiliated hospitals | [AHA Fast Facts on U.S. Hospitals, 2026](https://www.aha.org/statistics/fast-facts-us-hospitals) reports **3,567 community hospitals in a system**. | Evidence that coordinated staffing decisions can span many facilities. This is a different snapshot and unit of measurement from the 397 systems above. | 3,567 buyers, facilities using this workflow, or current demand for this product. |
| RN hiring pressure | [BLS Occupational Outlook, Registered Nurses](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/registered-nurses.htm) projects about **180,800 RN openings per year** on average during 2025–35 and reports about **3.5 million RN jobs in 2025**. | Context for the size and turnover of the occupation the first demo models. | That all openings require internal transfer planning or that our app fills them. |
| Workforce spend | [AHA, Higher Costs for the People Who Provide Care](https://www.aha.org/system/files/media/file/2026/03/Costs-of-Caring-2026.pdf) says hospitals spent **more than $1 trillion on dedicated health care workers in 2025** and workforce costs rose **5.6%** that year. | Explains why workforce decisions matter to health-system leaders. | Any cost savings or ROI attributable to this prototype. |
| Planning direction | [AHA 2026 Health Care Workforce Scan](https://www.aha.org/system/files/media/file/2025/12/2026-Health-Care-Workforce-Scan.pdf) describes upskilling, cross-training, workforce pools, and tools that reduce administrative burden. | Supports the premise that organizations are exploring mobility and career pathways. | A customer endorsement or proof that the specific product is adopted. |

## Narrow adoption hypothesis

1. **First workflow:** one planned RN opening. A central planner enters total and role-mix minima and reviews source-safe options with managers.
2. **First buyer hypothesis:** a system workforce or operations leader who owns planning efficiency and coverage risk. We have not interviewed this buyer or tested willingness to pay.
3. **Pilot:** compare 10 deidentified opening cases in the manual spreadsheet process and a secure version of the planner. Target at least 50% lower median time to a reviewer-accepted, policy-compliant plan with zero source-floor breaches in accepted plans. This is an unvalidated target, not a result.
4. **Expansion after evidence:** configure another job family, then integrate current roster, credential, schedule, and approval records. Customer-specific validated rules and decision history could eventually improve retention and differentiation; that advantage has not been established.

The static demo does not handle real employee or health data, compute a monetary TAM, or claim customer usage.
